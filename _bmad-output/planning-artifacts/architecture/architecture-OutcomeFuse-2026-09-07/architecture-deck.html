Architecture Spine

Twenty-one decisions, one paradigm, one build order. This deck is not a summary — it is a place to argue. Eight slides are marked PUSH BACK HERE because the call could reasonably have gone the other way.

What is fixed

Ports-and-adapters. Append-only decision log as system of record. Pure core.

What is arguable

AD-1, AD-3, AD-7, AD-10, AD-14, AD-16, AD-19, and the third host adapter.

How to use it

Press ? for keys, n for speaker notes. Stop me on any marked slide.

Notes

Set the frame before slide 2: we are not reviewing code, we are reviewing the small set of rules that stop two competent engineers building two incompatible systems. Every AD in here exists because a divergence was identified — not because it is good practice.

Ask up front for one thing: if you think an invariant is wrong, say so now. After E7 the protected core is running and these become expensive.

01 · The tension

Two requirements pull in opposite directions

FR50 · FR51 · FR52

Wrap an existing loop. No re-architecture. Drop OutcomeFuse into LangGraph, Agent Framework, an SDK loop — and take it out again to get a clean baseline arm.

Implies: sit lightly on the side. Observe. Do not own control flow.

FR1 · FR23 · FR2

Decide before every unit of work. Deny, substitute, gate, halt. Stop immediately on sufficiency — mid-loop, not at the next natural boundary.

Implies: be on the critical path. Own the verdict. Own termination.

Notes

The failure this slide is protecting against is concrete and it is in AD-1’s Prevents clause: one integration honours deny by raising, another by returning a sentinel. Both “work”. The decision records are then not comparable across hosts, and FR51’s two-dissimilar-frameworks claim quietly means nothing.

The worse variant: a host silently does not honour stop-sufficient. The run continues, burns budget, and files as halt-exhausted. That is the product’s headline event being misreported by an integration bug.

Do not skip ahead to AD-1 — let the room feel both pulls first, otherwise the mediated boundary looks like ceremony.

02 · The resolution PUSH BACK HERE

AD-1 — Mediated control boundary

The host adapter proposes a step and applies the returned verdict — including termination.

Interception and loop-driving are adapter strategies, not architectures. No host framework type may appear above the adapter layer.

Prevents Two integrations honouring deny differently — and a host silently not honouring stop-sufficient, refiling the headline event as halt-exhausted.

The alternative we rejected — and what it would cost you

Pure interception (hook the framework, never call us): cheapest to adopt, but the governor cannot terminate — it can only decline the next call. FR23’s immediate stop degrades to “stop at the next natural boundary”, and the sufficiency claim goes with it.

Drive the loop ourselves: total control, exact enforcement — and FR50 is dead. Every adoption becomes a re-architecture, FR52’s clean OFF arm becomes a fork, and we are competing with the frameworks instead of governing them.

Cost of what we chose: enforcement is cooperative. An adapter that lies produces a plausible, internally consistent, entirely false audit trail. That debt is paid at AD-15 / FR107, not here.

Notes

The word doing the work is mediated. We neither observe passively nor seize control: we define one narrow port and require the adapter to apply what comes back. That makes “how do we hook LangGraph?” an implementation question inside one directory instead of an architectural question with three answers.

Expect the objection “this is just a callback”. Yes — the architecture is in what is forbidden, not in the call. No framework type above the adapter layer means the core, the runtime, the harness and the record schema never learn which host produced a run, which is exactly what makes FR51’s cross-framework comparison meaningful.

If someone argues for interception, make them say out loud what happens to FR23. That is where it dies.

03 · The paradigm

Ports and adapters. Every arrow points inward.

Notes

Read the table in the spine alongside this: outcomefuse.core and outcomefuse.ports may depend on nothing in this project. That is enforceable with an import-linter rule, and it should be, on day one — the rule is worth more than the diagram.

Note where the harness sits: it depends on everything, including the store. That is deliberate. The harness is allowed to be the messy top of the stack because it is the only component allowed to compute a reportable figure (AD-10).

The viewer reads the store only — not the runtime, not the evidence store. AD-17 and AD-19 both lean on that single arrow.

04 · The system of record

AD-2 — The log is the truth. Run state is a fold.

Every decision is appended before it takes effect. Every state change is itself an appended event — budget reservation and settlement included.

Ledger position, quality_state, iteration count, decision history: all derived by folding the log. Never a second mutable truth kept in sync.

Anything not in the log did not happen and may not be claimed.

Prevents Mutable run state and a written log drifting apart, leaving no authority on what actually happened.

What it buys

Replay, audit and the benchmark harness stop being three subsystems and become three readers of one thing. Build the log well once; the other three are queries.

Notes

The clause people under-read is “every state change is itself an appended event, budget reservation and settlement included”. It is tempting to keep the ledger as an object and write summary rows. Do that and you have re-created the drift the AD exists to prevent, in the one place — money — where drift is a published-figure defect.

Replay equivalence is deliberately weaker than byte equality: identical decision sequence, identical reason codes, identical terminal reason, identical ledger totals. We never claim byte-identical model output and we should never let a slide imply we do.

Practical consequence for E2: the fold is production code with a test that folds a synthetic stream twice and gets the same state. It is not a debugging utility.

05 · The event order

One decision emits a fixed sequence

Reserve before act

Every spend is held before it happens — governor overhead included.

Cycles recur in place

An evidence cycle may appear wherever an advisor or the Gate needs one, including after outcome-observed.

Gate cadence is core-declared

Which step classes can change the candidate result is declared by the core — not judged per driver.

Notes

The last card is the one to dwell on. If each driver decided when the Gate runs, the same run could end halt-exhausted under one driver and stop-sufficient under another — and both logs would look internally correct. FR98’s cadence has to be a core constant for the terminal reason to mean anything.

Note the second evidence cycle: that is where the Quality Gate’s own model executions land, and under FR98/AD-4 their cost is debited as governor overhead attributed to quality-gate. Teams routinely forget the Gate costs money, then publish a saving that excludes the cost of proving the saving.

This slide is also the review checklist for any adapter PR: does the log come out in this order, every time?

06 · Ordering PUSH BACK HERE

AD-3 — One decision lane per run

Prevents Two concurrently proposed steps each passing affordability against the same remaining and breaching the ceiling — with no individual rule violated — and a partially ordered log that cannot be replayed deterministically.

The Ledger holds three quantities and never conflates them

A hold is released by exactly one of spend-settled, a denial, or run termination. Each settlement carries the id of the decision it settles, so host-side parallelism cannot make the fold ambiguous.

The alternative we rejected — and what it would cost you

Concurrent verdicts with an atomic ledger. Tempting: serial decisions put governor latency on the critical path of every step, and a wide batch waits on a queue. NFR1 is measured per decision but felt per batch.

What it costs: the log becomes partially ordered, so “identical decision sequence” — the whole definition of replay equivalence — has no meaning. Determinism would then need a total order reconstructed after the fact, which is a distributed-log problem we have not budgeted for. Mitigation instead: latency is measured against batch width, not per step alone, and execution stays parallel.

Notes

This is the most likely genuine performance objection in the room, so give it air. The honest position: we traded throughput for a total order because the total order is what makes every §8 claim reproducible, and reproducibility is the deliverable.

If someone proposes batch verdicts — decide N steps in one call — that is compatible with AD-3 and worth capturing. It keeps one lane, one order, and amortises the decision cost across the batch. It is not in scope but it is not foreclosed.

The Deferred section already says a distributed ledger with the same properties is a different design and nothing here forecloses it. Say that; it defuses the “this won’t scale” framing without conceding the MVP.

07 · Purity

AD-4 — Four single writers

Notes

The divergence being prevented is subtle and expensive: one mechanism debits its own spend, another lets the caller debit. Both satisfy FR4. But FR15’s per-mechanism attribution now has two meanings, and FR62’s ablation compares numbers computed differently. Nothing fails; the evidence quietly stops being evidence.

“An advisor never performs I/O and never mutates run state” is testable: the advisor test suite runs with no network and no model, full stop. If a mechanism cannot be written that way it is telling you it wants an EvidenceRequest.

Watch for the request to let the Quality Gate call the model directly “because it is protected”. No — FR98 gate executions go through the same request/fulfil path and get debited as overhead against quality-gate. That registry entry exists for exactly this.

08 · The subtlest idea in the deck

The EvidenceRequest cycle

EvidenceRequest is a closed, core-owned sum type with a declared outcome schema per kind — rubric judgement, compression pass, complexity estimate, confidence estimate, planner envelope. Re-invocations per decision are bounded and the bound is recorded in the manifest.

Notes

This is the mechanism that makes “pure advisors” survive contact with reality. An advisor that needs a model call does not get one — it returns a request describing what it needs, the driver fulfils it, appends the outcome, and calls the advisor again with the result in hand.

Three consequences worth stating explicitly: (1) replay is free, because the advisor is a function of recorded outcomes; (2) every piece of evidence has a cost that is recorded and attributed before it is used; (3) an advisor cannot smuggle in a side effect, because it has no way to perform one.

Why closed sum type: an open one means an advisor could invent a request kind whose outcome schema is unknown to the core, and replay would have nothing to validate against. The bound on re-invocations is the loop fuse for this cycle — without it a badly written advisor can spin the driver.

Shadow mode (AD-11) hangs off this: the shadow driver fulfils no request that would spend against the observed path.

09 · Attribution

A decomposition, not a winner

No tie-break elects a causer

Where several mechanisms contribute to one step’s overhead or avoided spend, the Ledger records an explicit decomposition over every contributor, summing to the total.

Elect a single winner and FR62’s breakdown becomes a function of the tie-break rule rather than of the mechanisms.

Composition order is core-owned and versioned

Proposals compose in a fixed order, recorded with the decision — never per-implementation. Two orders yield different total savings for the same run.

Disabled means not registered

No mechanism carries an if enabled branch. An FR62 ablation is a registry change and is byte-identical to having cut the mechanism.

Degraded is not disabled

A mechanism that raised is deregistered mid-run and the run is marked degraded (AD-20). That is a different thing from never having been enabled, and the record says which.

Gate executions are attributed to quality-gate — a named registry entry that exists for attribution and FR62 ablation even though the Gate itself is protected and never cut.

Notes

“Disabled means not registered” is the single highest-leverage line in AD-4 for the ablation claim. If mechanisms carry enable-flags, an ablation measures “the mechanism with its branch not taken”, which is not the same system as one without the mechanism — and the difference is unbounded once the flag sits in a hot path.

The attribution decomposition matters most in the outcome the cut order makes likeliest: if the schedule collapses to the protected core, the ablation will show how much of the saving is exact tool deduplication alone. A tie-break rule would let us hide that. A decomposition cannot.

The composition-order point is easy to lose in review. Ordering advisors differently is not a refactor — it changes the published number. It is versioned and stamped on the decision for that reason.

Twenty-one decisions, six jobs

Grouped by what they stop, not by number. Each AD’s Prevents clause is the reason it exists — if you cannot state the divergence, the rule is ceremony.

11 · Group 1

Ordering & state

AD-2 — The decision log is the system of record
Prevents Mutable run state and a written log drifting apart, leaving no authority on what actually happened.
  • Appended before it takes effect.
  • Every state change is an event — reservation and settlement included.
  • Run state is fold(log).
  • Replay equivalence: same sequence, same reason codes, same terminal reason, same ledger totals.
AD-3 — One decision lane per run
Prevents Two concurrent proposals each passing affordability against the same remaining and breaching the ceiling, with no individual rule violated.
  • Verdicts serial; execution parallel.
  • verification_reserve / in_flight / spent never conflated.
  • Hold released by exactly one of: settle, deny, terminate.
  • Latency measured against batch width.

The through-line

These two exist to make one sentence true: “the same run, replayed, produces the same decisions.” Drop either and FR68, NFR3 and every comparison in §8 lose their oracle.

Notes

Worth naming the class of bug AD-3 catches, because it is the kind that survives code review: no rule is violated, each check passes, and the ceiling is breached anyway. Concurrency bugs in a budget system do not look like bugs, they look like arithmetic.

“Byte equality of model output is not required and is never claimed” — keep repeating this. It is the difference between a defensible replay claim and one a reviewer can break in thirty seconds with a temperature setting.

12 · Group 2

Purity & boundaries

AD-4 — Four single writers
Prevents One mechanism debiting its own spend while another lets the caller debit — both obeying FR4, yet giving FR15 two meanings.
AD-5 — Redaction at the port
Prevents Sensitive payloads reaching the durable record, where a stack trace or rendered artifact leaks what the sink was supposed to strip.

Raw prompts, tool args and results are a transient working set. Only three declared core operations may see them: canonicalise-to-key, compress-to-capsule, verify-criterion. What crosses into the record is derived: scores, envelopes, keys, hashes, references.

AD-19 — Evidence store separate from the log
Prevents The blind-review packet being rendered from the same store that carries the gate verdict — silently invalidating false-sufficiency rate, the counter-metric the headline rests on.

Driver is sole writer. Harness is sole reader. Verifiers never touch it. The FR69 packet renderer is structurally denied the verdict, the decision record and the manifest.

Lifetime and access matrix on slide 20.

Notes

AD-5 and AD-19 look like two privacy rules. They are not. AD-5 is about what may enter the durable record at all; AD-19 is about keeping the one artifact a human blind-reviews contaminated-free. Different failure, different fix, both structural rather than procedural.

“Structurally denied” means the renderer is not given a handle to the verdict — not that it is asked not to look. If the blind-review packet can see the gate verdict, false-sufficiency rate measures our agreement with ourselves.

The one legitimate raw-content exception: an evidence capsule preserves attributable facts verbatim (FR38), so it is raw and lives in the evidence store; the log carries its reference and hash, never its body.

13 · Group 3

Identity & hashing

AD-6 — One canonicaliser
Prevents The Tool Governor and the harness canonicalising differently — which fails silently, as cache misses that should have been hits, or a spurious rubric-drift refusal on a rubric that never changed.

One core-owned function serves every hash: contract identity (FR10), tool-call keys (FR29), progress fingerprint (FR27), result digests, rubric and answer-key freeze (FR65), baseline freeze (FR64).

Typed model → canonical JSON → SHA-256. Normalisation is specified and versioned: sorted keys, no insignificant whitespace, one numeric form so 1.0 and 1 cannot hash differently.

Artifacts that are source rather than structure — the verifier tests, the baseline definition script — take the one other admissible route: a file-digest manifest of sorted relative path plus SHA-256 of file bytes, itself canonicalised and hashed by the same function. Nothing else may hash by any other means.

Every hashed artifact declares its route id, so two freeze tools cannot silently pick different routes for the same artifact. Within the file-digest route: POSIX separators, NFC normalisation, byte-sorted order, LF line endings — a Windows freeze and a Linux freeze that disagree would fire exactly the drift refusal this decision exists to prevent.

AD-8 — Record schema and reason registry are owned here
Prevents FR104’s promise — that a published code still means what it meant — being delegated to an external specification that can revoke it.

OpenTelemetry GenAI attribute names may be borrowed as convention. The schema shall not depend on a spec that is still Development-status with no published releases.

Published reason codes are never redefined, repurposed or removed. Every decision row carries the governor’s own wall-clock decision cost, so NFR1 is measured from the record.

Notes

The 1.0 versus 1 example is not pedantry — it is the actual bug. The freeze tool runs before the governor exists, probably in a script, probably with a different serialiser default. Two hashes, one rubric, and FR65 fires a drift refusal on something nobody changed.

The second route exists because AD-7 requires the verifier tests to be frozen with the rubric, and a test file is not a structure a typed model can validate. With only the typed-model route that clause was literally unsatisfiable. Two routes, both ending in the same function — and the door closes there, because every artifact names the route it takes rather than leaving a freeze tool to choose one. Path separators, Unicode form, ordering and line endings are pinned inside the file-digest route for the same reason: the drift refusal cannot tell a rubric that changed from a rubric hashed on a different operating system.

AD-8 is a scope-discipline decision as much as a technical one. Borrowing OTel names costs nothing and buys familiarity; depending on OTel means our stability promise inherits someone else’s. Deferred says we adopt the export later as an additive mapping, which is the right shape.

The decision-cost-on-every-row detail is quietly excellent: no separate instrumentation to keep honest, and NFR1 becomes a query over the log.

14 · Group 4 PUSH BACK HERE

Safety

AD-7 — A contract executes no code
Prevents Remote code execution driven by a data file — in a product whose position is that platform teams hand contracts to agent owners.

FR8’s “executable deterministic verification method” is a selection from a closed, project-owned registry, parameterised declaratively. No import path, expression or callable crosses the contract boundary. Safe loader only, bounded size and depth. A verifier is a pure function of its declared arguments — citation-resolves is handed the run’s derived citable index by its caller and never fetches it, never touches the evidence store.

That index is pinned too, because an unshaped argument moves the divergence rather than closing it: a core-owned typed model built by the driver, canonicalised and hashed under AD-6, its hash appended before any verifier is invoked. Two implementations deriving it differently would reach different verdicts — and different terminal reasons — on the same run.

Purity is what makes the freeze buildable: every verifier is testable against fixtures alone, needing neither the record spine nor a live run.

Verifier typeMode
field-present, type-is, numeric-range,
set-membership, regex-match		constraint-backed
exact-match-against-answer-key,
citation-resolves		reference-backed

Mode is a property of the type, not a per-contract assertion. That is what stops reference-backed being claimed for a pass that never touched a known-correct value.

FR108 — breadth is settled before the freeze, not guessed at

  • One real Outcome Contract authored per committed workload first.
  • Every intended criterion classified E expressible · N new deterministic type needed · A advisory — explicitly covering the semantic ones that are easiest to miss: root-cause, query-result and code-location correctness, evidence completeness, and whether a conclusion is supported rather than merely present.
  • At most three further deterministic types admitted, each reusable, declarative, I/O-free, mode-fixed by the registry and covered by positive, negative, boundary, malformed-input and replay tests.
  • A per-workload coverage report — reference-backed versus constraint-backed mandatory counts — published with the freeze. Predominantly constraint-backed must be reported as such.
  • The registry, its tests and the report are hashed in the same operation as the rubric and answer keys. A rubric whose criteria take their meaning from an unfrozen registry is not frozen. The tests hash through AD-6’s file-digest manifest — they are source, not structure.

semantic-match, llm-judge, root-cause-quality and citation-supports-claim stay barred unless reducible to deterministic comparison against frozen structured reference data.

AD-12 — Approval is a port with a scripted decider
Prevents FR89’s fail-closed-on-unavailable being asserted rather than exercised, FR100’s timeout cases needing a human in front of a reproducibility harness, and — the sharper one — a plain timeout being misrouted as an unavailability.

channel-unavailable and no-response are distinct states and shall not be merged. This one was a genuine defect in the previous draft — next slide.

AD-18 — The quality floor is protected by the Policy
Prevents The floor carve-out living inside a pluggable estimator, so swapping the estimator silently removes the one rule the PRD calls non-negotiable.

The estimator may only ever propose low-value. The Policy rejects any such proposal against an unmet mandatory criterion, and the attempt is recorded as a violation, not a saving (FR99).

AD-20 — Failure posture is placed, not chosen per site

Fail-open is a property of the advisor registry — an advisor that raises is deregistered for the run and a degraded event is appended. Fail-closed is a property of the driver — gate unavailability, ledger loss and approval-channel unavailability terminate through the FR2 ladder; an approval timeout does not. The registry is run-scoped, so a transient failure cannot degrade every later repeat in the same process.

AD-21 — Non-synthetic data is refused, never inherited
Prevents Production, confidential or customer traffic silently inheriting a retention profile designed for data the builder authored.

mvp-synthetic-v1 is admissible for synthetic only, and replayed inherits the class of the run it replays. Until a production-data governance profile is approved, the runtime refuses to persist evidence and decision log alike rather than falling back. Detail on slide 20.

AD-7 — the alternative we rejected, and what it would cost you

An open verifier registry, or a sandboxed expression language. It would let a contract express anything a criterion needs, today, without a registry PR. The closed set is genuinely limiting: seven types, and only two of them reference-backed.

What it would cost: the contract is a data file handed between teams. Any callable reference is an RCE vector and any network-capable verifier is an exfiltration channel straight through AD-5 — while also breaking core purity and FR68 replay. A sandbox is not a small project, and its escape hatches are the whole risk. Q-B is now closed: breadth is no longer guessed at, because FR108 forces four real contracts and a classified criterion review before the freeze, admits at most three demonstrated additions, and publishes a coverage report that makes a predominantly constraint-backed workload say so.

Notes

Q-B used to be the honest hole in this slide. It closed on 2026-09-09 by turning the question into a gate: write the contracts, classify every criterion E / N / A, allow three additions at most, publish the coverage report, and hash the registry with the rubric. The risk did not disappear — it acquired a place in the schedule and a number attached to it, which is the most you can do with an unknown before you know it.

The coverage report is the part to defend. FR21’s run-level qualifier hides how much of a floor is substantive: a workload can pass every mandatory criterion on field-present and type-is alone. Reporting the split is what stops that reading as independently established semantic correctness — and AD-10 refuses to publish a workload result without it.

AD-18 is the one to defend hardest if anyone proposes “just make the estimator smarter”. Pluggability is fine; where the veto lives is not. The veto is in the Policy so that swapping estimators cannot remove it, and the rejected proposal is recorded so FR99 can report attempted floor breaches as violations.

AD-20’s run-scoped registry catches a nasty one: without it, a transient advisor failure in repeat 3 degrades repeats 4 through 20 in the same process, and FR58’s repeated runs quietly stop being repeats.

15 · A defect this review found

AD-12 — a timeout is not an outage

What the rule said before 2026-09-09

AD-12 named one failure state. Approval-channel unavailability and approval timeout were carried by the same clause — so an adapter author reading it had no way to tell that FR89 and FR95 are different requirements with different outcomes.

What it says now

Two states, distinct and never merged, because FR2 ranks them differently. The MVP ApprovalPort implementation is unchanged — a scripted decider driven by the case definition (approve · deny · never respond) — so FR100’s timeout cases run in a harness with no human sitting in front of it.

Notes

Show this one deliberately rather than skipping past it. It is the clearest evidence that the Prevents discipline earns its keep: the rule was already written and already reviewed, and it still carried a conflation that would have shipped as an adapter behaviour nobody could see in the log — because the log would have said fail-closed and been internally consistent about it.

The asymmetry is what makes it dangerous rather than merely wrong. Fail-closed sits above the approval gate in FR2’s ladder, so the misroute always lands on the more severe terminal reason. A run that should have applied its contract’s on_timeout halts instead, and the defect reads as conservatism.

AD-20 carries the matching half and now says so explicitly: gate-verdict unavailability, ledger-state loss and approval-channel unavailability go through the ladder — an approval timeout does not, and is governed here.

Concrete ask for the room: this is exactly the class of thing the conformance battery should catch. “Approval pause observed” is one scenario today; it wants to be two.

16 · Group 5

Evidence & honesty

AD-9 — Every run opens with a manifest
Prevents Harness, adapter and viewer each deciding independently what a run’s configuration was — surfacing later as an unexplainable difference between two runs believed identical.

Run id, mode, data_class — from the frozen case-set attestation, no default, an absent class refuses the run — and the retention profile it selects, contract hash, rubric hash, verifier-registry version and hash, coverage-report hash, frozen baseline hash, case-set identity, model ids and provider versions, pinned cost-table version, route, streaming disabled, enabled-mechanism registry, adapter version, code version, sqlite3 library version, seed.

No comparison may be published from a run without a manifest.

Preregistration is content-anchored

Targets, counter-metric thresholds, minimum case counts and blind-review sample size are written as their own hashed, timestamped artifact. Every evaluation-set run’s manifest carries that record’s hash.

FR102’s ordering guarantee is anchored to content, not to a timestamp asserted by the interested party. The harness refuses any comparison where evaluation execution precedes preregistration.

AD-16 — One workspace database, sealed per run
Prevents Four consumers disagreeing about where the record lives or whether it can be edited after the fact.

SQLite: one canonical database per benchmark workspace, holding many runs, on a writer discipline that is imposed rather than assumed — library ≥ 3.51.3, one writer per database file under an OS advisory lock and a second writer refused, not retried, BEGIN IMMEDIATE, explicit busy timeout, synchronous=FULL, local storage only. The writer exposes append only. Each row holds the hash of its predecessor within its own run; that run’s terminal hash is the run seal, cited by every proof card.

Notes

The preregistration-hash detail is the strongest anti-self-deception device in the architecture and it is easy to skate past. A timestamp proves nothing when the party asserting it is the party with the incentive. A hash carried inside every evaluation-run manifest proves the targets existed in that exact form before the run.

“No comparison may be published from a run without a manifest” is enforced in one place — the harness — and that is deliberate under AD-10: no other component re-derives reportability.

17 · Reportability PUSH BACK HERE

AD-10 — Three gates, and only the harness evaluates them

The baseline arm is built by nobody

FR52’s OFF is an adapter state, not a driver. With the governor out of the call path entirely, the baseline arm shares no governor code with the governed arm — so its latency is not governor-inflated and §8.1’s fairness commitment is structural, not a promise.

The alternative we rejected — and what it would cost you

Label everything, refuse nothing. Softer, keeps every run, lets the team see partial results early. And it is how measurement integrity dies: a demo-convenience label becomes a footnote, the footnote becomes small print, and the headline is quoted without it.

The split is deliberate. Refuse where a defect makes the number meaningless (streaming, non-conformant adapter, mismatched manifests, missing counter-metric). Label where the number is real but weaker than claimed (metering unavailable → self-reported, shadow → projected, degraded run → degraded). Cost of our choice: some runs are simply thrown away, and that will be annoying at 2am.

Notes

Streaming is barred on the evidence path for a specific reason worth saying out loud: APIM estimates both prompt and completion tokens when stream: true. FR79 would then be a reconciliation between two guesses, and F15 — the whole gateway-metering feature — would buy nothing.

“Manifest-identical except for the run id and the fields the comparison exists to vary” is the sharpest tool in the whole document. Field-by-field comparison, refuse on any other difference — route, model version, cost-table version, seed, adapter version. Most published agent-cost comparisons in the wild would fail that check.

Also note who evaluates: the harness alone. No other component re-derives any gate. If the viewer or an adapter starts computing reportability, we have three answers again.

The accompaniment list is exhaustive and worth reading out: no headline without its per-mechanism breakdown, no tool-call reduction without suppression accuracy, nothing below its minimum case count, no gross figure as headline, no successes without the failures, no counter-metric without its preregistered threshold — and no workload result without its verification-mode coverage report, carrying the predominantly constraint-backed label where AD-7’s condition holds.

18 · Integrity PUSH BACK HERE

AD-16 — Append-only is discipline. The seal on the proof card is the mechanism.

One SQLite database per benchmark workspace, holding many runs. The writer exposes append only — no UPDATE, no DELETE against the decision table — and the concurrency discipline is imposed, not assumed, because WAL surfaces contention as SQLITE_BUSY rather than queueing: library ≥ 3.51.3 asserted at open, one writer per database file under an OS advisory lock taken at store open, a second writer refused rather than retried — an in-process lock is void the moment the harness forks a subprocess per case, which is a legal implementation — BEGIN IMMEDIATE so a transaction cannot upgrade mid-flight and lose its busy retry, an explicit busy timeout, synchronous=FULL, and local storage only — the wal-index does not work over a network filesystem.

What the chain proves, bounded honestly: VACUUM and checkpointing rewrite pages but never row values, so neither breaks it — but a seal living only in the same file proves nothing against a rewrite-and-recompute. Tamper-evidence is load-bearing only because proof cards carry the seals outside the database. It establishes neither authorship nor time.

Retention (NFR12) follows sensitivity, not file layout: the redacted log is the long-lived tier, archived or deleted per workspace; raw evidence is the short-lived tier, deleted per run. Next slide.

The alternative we rejected — and what it would cost you

“It’s append-only because we don’t write UPDATE.” That is a code-review convention, not a property. Anyone with the file has sqlite3, and the party asserting the timestamps is the party §8.1 names as interested.

A real append-only store (ledger DB, WORM object storage, external notary) would give a stronger guarantee — and adds an operational dependency, a service to run, and a second place a run can fail, for an MVP with two environments and no staging tier. The chain is the cheap 80%: tampering does not become impossible, it becomes detectable. Push back if you think detectable is not enough for what §8 claims.

Notes

Be precise about the limit, because a reviewer will find it: a hash chain we compute ourselves does not stop us regenerating the whole chain, and it says nothing about who wrote a row or when. It stops selective edits, and the seal only becomes a citation once it has left the file — which is why every proof card carries the seals of the runs it compares. External notarisation is the answer if we ever need a third party to trust the log without trusting us — a post-MVP conversation, worth flagging as such.

This changed on 2026-09-09 and the change is worth naming rather than quietly restating. One database per run made retention a filesystem operation and removed every locking question — but it scattered the record across hundreds of files for a benchmark whose whole job is cross-run comparison, and it tied the retention story to the file layout rather than to what the rows contain. One workspace database with a per-run chain keeps the seal exactly as strong, because the chain never depended on the file boundary.

The concurrency point is the one to say slowly. AD-3 serialises decisions within a run; it says nothing about two runs. The harness will execute runs in parallel, and WAL by itself only reports the contention — so the store owns a real writer discipline: one advisory-locked writer per file, BEGIN IMMEDIATE, a busy timeout, synchronous=FULL. The lock is an OS lock rather than an in-process one, because a harness that forks a subprocess per case gets N writers and N in-process locks that know nothing about each other. The version floor is not housekeeping either: the WAL-reset corruption bug runs from 3.7.0 to 3.51.2 and triggers on precisely this workload.

19 · Lifetime PUSH BACK HERE

AD-19 & AD-21 — a declared expiry, and a profile that may not be inherited

Retention profile mvp-synthetic-v1

  • Raw evidence: expires at run_closed_at + 60 days, deleted as a whole evidence/<run_id>/ directory — deliverables, capsules, raw tool outputs, suppression and fidelity artifacts, generated blind-review packets, temporaries.
  • Redacted records and proof artifacts: 180 days from campaign seal — and a campaign is a workspace, one thing under two words. Seal is a harness command, executed once, refused while any run is unsealed; after it the store refuses to open a writer, so nothing can join a sealed workspace. max(run_closed_at) ≤ campaign_sealed_at is asserted at seal, which is what makes the redacted tier outlive the raw tier.
  • Run closure is an appended event, never an update — AD-16 forbids UPDATE, and an unsealed run has no terminal row to stamp anyway. The driver appends run-closed, which is the run’s terminal row and therefore its seal; the sweep appends run-abandoned to whatever is left, which both seals it and gives it an expiry. No column is ever mutated, and never a filesystem modification, access or copy time.
  • No per-run extension. Evidence that expires before an FR69 / FR70 / FR71 assessment completes is regenerated by re-running the frozen case.
  • Deletion writes a content-free receipt — run id, evidence-manifest hash, profile, scheduled expiry, deleted-at, result — into a separate append-only campaign retention manifest, and never into any run’s hash chain, so no act of retention can alter a seal.

Logical deletion appropriate to synthetic data. It is not cryptographic erasure and shall not be described as such.

AD-21 — Non-synthetic data is refused, never inherited
Prevents Production, confidential or customer traffic silently inheriting a profile designed for data the builder authored — the most likely way this system acquires a compliance problem, precisely because it requires nobody to decide anything.

Every run declares a data_class, supplied by the frozen case-set attestation with no default — an absent class refuses the run. mvp-synthetic-v1 is admissible for synthetic only, and replayed inherits the class of the run it replays: replaying captured production traffic does not launder it. Where the class is anything other than synthetic and no approved production profile exists, the runtime refuses to persist — evidence and decision log alike. Refusing only the evidence would leave the record with no permitted profile, which is NFR12 unmet by a narrower route; keying the refusal on a non-synthetic label would let replayed walk straight past. FR106 stands until that profile exists.

Verifiers are handed the run’s derived citable index by their caller (AD-7) — a core-owned typed model the driver builds and hashes before any verifier runs; they never read the store, and never fetch anything. The evidence root stays gitignored and out of generated HTML, the submission archive, test snapshots and application logs.

The alternative we rejected — and what it would cost you

An extension request per run. Humane — and it is how every retention policy dies. The extension gets granted at exactly the moment someone wants the evidence, which is exactly the moment the rule was written for. We chose regeneration from the frozen case instead: more compute, no discretion.

Sixty and one hundred and eighty days are judgements, not derivations. They are declared so they can be argued with — an undeclared lifetime cannot be wrong, which is precisely what was wrong with the previous draft. Push back if the assessment cycle runs longer than sixty days in practice.

Notes

This slide closes Q-C, which was open at the last session with exactly the framing “AD-19 requires retention-bounded under NFR12 and names no period”. The fix is not really the number. It is that expiry now derives from a recorded event with an owner — the driver appends run-closed, which is the terminal row, and the sweep appends run-abandoned to whatever is still unsealed when the campaign seals — rather than from the filesystem; that deletion is a whole directory rather than a selective sweep; and that the receipt is content-free and lands outside every run’s chain so it cannot alter a seal.

Two details are worth saying explicitly because they were nearly left implicit. Closure is an append, not a column write: AD-16 forbids UPDATE, so anything that stamps a timestamp onto an existing row is already out of spec, and an unsealed run has no terminal row to stamp in the first place. And seal is a command someone runs, once, refused while any run is unsealed. Left unowned, one harness would seal when its case set finished and another lazily at the first sweep — and either could then admit a new run whose raw evidence outlived the redacted tier describing it, inverting the two tiers this slide calls safe by definition.

The access matrix is where the blind-review guarantee actually lives. “Structurally denied” back on the purity slide is this one row: the renderer gets evidence and the contract, and no handle to the verdict, the decision record or the manifest. If it could see any of the three, false-sufficiency rate measures our agreement with ourselves — and that is the counter-metric the headline claim rests on.

AD-21 is the one to say out loud even though it changes nothing today. Refusing is deliberately more annoying than inheriting; the annoyance is the mechanism. Note where the refusal is keyed: on the class being anything other than synthetic, not on a non-synthetic label — because replayed inherits the class of the run it replays, and a label-keyed check would wave replayed production straight through. The profile it demands has to settle tenant and use-case partitioning, storage location, encryption in transit and at rest, workload and operator identities, role-based read/write/delete, legal classification, retention by data class, deletion SLA and verification, backup and replica deletion, incident handling, audit access and export restrictions — before a single non-synthetic run persists anything.

Anticipate “this is premature for an MVP”. The counter is that the rule costs one refusal path now and prevents the one failure that arrives silently: nobody decides to use production data, someone just points a shadow run at it.

Modes & surfaces

AD-11 — Shadow is a driver, not a flag
Prevents A shadow branch inside the Policy, inside every mechanism, and — because FR91 exempts shadow from fail-closed — inside the fail-closed paths. A conditional in the most safety-critical code, on the branch least exercised by tests.

AD-15 — Adapters pass conformance or produce nothing

Deny honoured · substitution applied · approval pause observed · sufficiency stop terminates · fail-closed halts · shadow not applied — asserted against the decision log, never adapter internals. Plus FR107’s out-of-band side-effect probe: scripted tools assert for themselves whether they were invoked, because a falsified log cannot detect its own falsification.

AD-13 — The model seam is owned

LiteLLM sits behind a project-owned ModelPort. No LiteLLM type, exception or cost table appears above it. The cost table is pinned and recorded in the manifest — a pricing change between calibration and evaluation would silently move the cost figure.

AD-17 — The side-by-side view is generated, not served

One self-contained static HTML file per comparison. Reads the decision log and proof card only, never the evidence store. Computes no proof figures. Autoescaping enabled explicitly at environment construction — it is opt-in, and record content is untrusted. There is no running viewer process, so FR77’s “no path to influence execution” is structural.

AD-14 — the alternative we rejected, and what it would cost you

A persistent, cross-run tool cache. It looks like a free win and it is a real post-MVP lever. What it costs today: FR68 re-executability starts depending on cache warmth, two supposedly identical runs differ, FR64’s frozen baseline is no longer a fixed thing, and NFR6 tenant partitioning lands on the hot path. Run-scoped discharges NFR6 structurally — a run belongs to one tenant and one use case, so cross-user reuse is impossible by construction.

Notes

AD-11 is the cleanest example of the deck’s recurring move: a behavioural difference becomes a different component, not a flag. Same for FR52’s OFF being an adapter state and for “disabled means not registered”. If someone proposes a boolean, ask what the boolean’s false branch is tested by.

FR96’s first divergence is observed by the driver, not computed by a mechanism — the driver knows which decision it declined to apply, and no advisor has to infer it.

On AD-14: the persistent cache is in Deferred with two conditions attached — a tenant-partitioned key, and an answer for how FR64 and FR68 stay honest when warmth varies. Anyone arguing for it now needs to bring those two answers.

Two environments. Three gateway constraints that bite.

APIM tier must support llm-token-limit

Unavailable on Consumption. Its absence silently downgrades every run to FR80 self-reported. A working APIM instance is a critical-path dependency before any evaluation-set run.

The count is combined

Metrics silently drop

Streaming is banned on the evidence path

With stream: true APIM estimates both token counts. FR79 becomes a reconciliation between two guesses and F15 buys nothing. This is an admissibility refusal, not a warning.

Notes

22 · Build order

The order the architecture forces — not the order the numbers suggest

At the end of E7 the protected core runs.

Everything after is evidence, surfaces, or optimisation.

E3 moved, and it moved for a reason.

Registry breadth cannot be reviewed against contracts that do not exist yet, and a rubric cannot freeze over a registry that is still moving. So: draft (E1a), build and review the registry (E3), then freeze both together (E1b).

E9 is what makes the headline falsifiable.

Without it the savings number is unfalsifiable and §8.3 forbids publishing it. The evidence apparatus is as protected as the runtime.

Notes

Four ordering rules and what each costs if broken. E0 before E1a: freeze hashes and runtime hashes disagree, and FR65’s drift refusal fires on an unchanged rubric. E1a before E3: there are no real contracts to run the FR108 coverage review against, so registry breadth is guessed — which is the thing Q-B was open about. E3 before E1b: the rubric freezes over a registry that is still moving, so the criteria are not actually frozen. E1b before E2–E7: the rubric was written by someone who had seen the optimizer, and §8.2’s whole defence evaporates.

Two more that people try to break. E6 before E10: written after the adapters, the conformance battery becomes a description of whatever they already do instead of a specification they were written against. E8 before any published number: no manifest, no preregistration, no admissibility check — the figure is unciteable under §8.4.

The uncomfortable reading from BUILD-ORDER is worth reading aloud: if the schedule collapses to the protected core, we ship exact tool deduplication, a sufficiency stop and a loop fuse — and E8’s ablation will show precisely how much of the saving came from deduplication alone. §11.2’s attribution risk is made more likely by the cut order, not less.

What the cut order cannot remove: E0, E1a, E3, E1b, E2, E8 and E9. §8 makes the claim itself a deliverable, so the evidence apparatus is as protected as the runtime.

23 · The least obvious thing in the architecture

E0 → E1a → E3 → E1b. Then, and only then, the governor.

Why the canonicaliser is first

The freeze tool runs before the governor exists. Two processes — a freeze script and a runtime import — must produce identical hashes for the same structure, including for 1.0 versus 1.

Build it second and the rubric you froze cannot be verified against the rubric the harness later reads. FR65’s drift refusal fires on a rubric nobody changed, and the only fix is re-freezing after seeing the governor — precisely what §8.2 exists to prevent.

E1 is the cheapest epic to defer and the most expensive to have deferred

Nothing in it needs the governor to exist. All of it becomes untrustworthy once the governor does. Author the four workloads’ cases, write the answer keys and rubric, hash them, freeze the baseline definition, and split calibration from the sealed evaluation set as separate artifacts.

Why E1 split into E1a and E1b

FR108 put a review between them. Breadth cannot be reviewed without real contracts, and the freeze must include the registry, its tests and the coverage report — hashed in the same operation as the rubric. Anything less is a rubric frozen over a moving target.

Notes

This is the slide to make someone repeat back. It is not a preference and it is not a testing convention — it is the only ordering under which the headline claim is defensible, and it is invisible from the PRD alone. It falls out of §8.2 plus FR65 plus AD-6, and now FR108, together.

Sealed means sealed: no governed result may be executed against the evaluation set or inspected before preregistration. The case definitions are authored and known; it is the results that must remain unseen.

The E1a/E1b split is new and it is the practical consequence of closing Q-B. Before, “E1 freeze” was one block and the registry was assumed adequate. Now the freeze has a prerequisite that produces evidence — four contracts, a classification of every criterion, at most three additions, a published coverage report — and only then does anything get hashed.

If the team pushes to start on the governor because it is the interesting part, the counter is cheap: E0, E1a, E3 and E1b are days, and they are the epics that cannot be recovered later at any price.

24 · Scope PUSH BACK HERE

Three host adapters, where FR51 needs two

What is committed

langgraph 1.2.11 — classifiers only to 3.13 agent-framework 1.17.0 openai-agents 0.22.0 — pre-1.0, pin exactly

FR51 requires two dissimilar frameworks. Three were committed with no declared way to drop one — which is exactly the failure mode a cut order exists to prevent. The PRD was amended on 2026-09-07: the third adapter is now cut position 2.

The alternative — and what the third adapter costs you

Keep all three regardless. A third framework is real evidence of portability and it is the demo that convinces a sceptic. But it does not add an argument FR51 does not already have at two — and it multiplies the benchmark matrix: a wrapper, a conformance passage, four workloads of tools rebound, a share of the frozen FR64 baseline, and a share of FR100’s failure cases.

It is also the riskiest of the three: pre-1.0, pinned exactly, no compatibility promise. Push back if you think two dissimilar frameworks will read as a narrow claim — that is a legitimate position, and the answer is to pick the two most dissimilar, not to add a third.

Every adapter is admitted the same way

By passing E6’s conformance battery — never by its own unit tests. A hand-rolled ReAct reference adapter is built first and is the conformance reference.

FR107 — the probe that made the battery honest

Enforcement is cooperative by AD-1’s construction. An adapter that executes a gated or denied call while recording a clean pause produces a plausible, internally consistent and entirely false audit trail — and every claim in §8 then rests on it.

A decision record cannot detect the one failure it is the evidence for. So the battery asserts against the decision record and carries an out-of-band side-effect probe: scripted tools assert for themselves whether they were invoked.

Cut order, top of the list

Position 1: the static viewer (E14). Position 2: the third adapter. Neither touches anything the claim rests on. E0, E1, E2, E8 and E9 cannot be cut — §8 makes the claim itself a deliverable.

Notes

Tell the story of how FR107 came to exist: the spine and the PRD diverged, and closing the divergence surfaced work that was in no feature and no estimate. That is the argument for doing this reconciliation at all — it found a hole in the evidence chain, not just a documentation mismatch.

The line to land: a log cannot be its own integrity check when the component under test is the one writing the events. Everything else in the battery is assertion-against-the-record, which is right; this one case needs a witness outside it.

Also flag the langgraph 3.14 validation item — it declares classifiers only to 3.13 and we are pinning Python 3.14. Validate before committing the adapter, not during E10.
